# Complexity deferral is safe only when its sufficiency is falsifiable and observable

Qiven should distinguish disciplined staging from engineering luck.

A staged design is healthy when the simpler mechanism is an explicit hypothesis with known failure modes, observable signals, a test that can falsify the hypothesis, a frozen revisit trigger, and a credible migration path. A design is relying on luck when "not necessary", "too complex", or "we can add it later" substitutes for those things.

This distinction matters most in foundational systems with silent false negatives. A compiler failure, crashed process, or red CI gate is visible. A cognition retrieval miss may be invisible: the model can continue with a plausible answer while omitting the most relevant prior fact. Silent epistemic failures therefore justify stronger redundancy, retrieval diversity, and adversarial acceptance than ordinary convenience tooling.

Minimalism remains valuable, but only as **reversible simplicity with measured sufficiency**. Minimalism must not become permission to underbuild a foundation whose failure cannot be observed reliably.

When Qiven defers a materially deeper mechanism, future architecture work should ask:

- What exact hypothesis says the simpler mechanism is enough?
- What evidence would prove that hypothesis false?
- Is that failure signal visible before damage propagates?
- Is the acceptance test actually testing the claimed property?
- For high-blast-radius uncertainty, have we compared or shadow-tested the deeper alternative instead of dismissing it by intuition?

The Python-memory retrieval incident is a concrete example: deterministic retrieval and cold-boot acceptance existed, but the task transition did not force retrieval and the acceptance suite did not establish open-set recall. The resulting plausible reimplementation showed that passing the existing tests was not evidence for the stronger property we had implicitly assumed.
