# Keep Qiven Foundation small, foundational, and downstream-demand-driven

## Context
Foundation sits below the rest of the Qiven native ecosystem. Every abstraction, dependency, hidden cost, or semantic ambiguity introduced here propagates upward. Convenience therefore has a much higher architectural cost in Foundation than in product or domain code.

## Decision
Foundation contains only low-level reusable C++ primitives that are genuinely foundational, broadly useful below product/domain layers, and capable of explicit ownership, failure behavior, portability, predictable cost, and narrow dependencies.

Dependencies point downward. Foundation does not become a miscellaneous utilities repository, does not absorb product/domain logic, does not wrap every OS or standard-library facility, and does not add third-party dependencies without specific architectural justification. New primitives are introduced in response to concrete downstream need rather than speculative completeness.

## Alternatives Considered
Growing Foundation as a general-purpose utility collection; proactively adding generic helpers for hypothetical future use; accepting convenience dependencies because higher layers could otherwise reimplement them.

## Consequences
Foundation remains intentionally smaller than the set of things Qiven can build. Some conveniences stay in higher layers until repeated demand demonstrates a stable foundational abstraction. Review must actively resist abstraction creep.

## Revisit Conditions
Revisit the placement of a primitive when multiple downstream consumers demonstrate a stable shared requirement that can satisfy Foundation's ownership, failure, portability, dependency, and cost laws.

## Provenance
Genesis reconstruction from the committed Foundation architecture, README, and agent contract. The architecture document explicitly states that convenience is insufficient justification and that Foundation should remain smaller than Qiven's total capability surface.
